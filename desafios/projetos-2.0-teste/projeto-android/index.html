THE HISTORY OF ANDROID

Probably you've heard about Android's little green mascot, but did you know it has a name? Its name is Bugdroid. Over the years, many people have wondered who created Bugdroid, but the truth is that very few know the story behind its creation.

THE FIRST VERSION

The first version of the mascot was created in 2007 and was developed by Dan Morrill . It featured a much more robotic appearance and became known as the Dandroids, because it represented both Android and Android's developer community. The goal was simply to create a symbol that anyone could easily recognize.

This first version became known as a tribute to the Android developers and their creators, the Dandroids

A NEW MASCOT EMERGES

The idea of creating a new mascot gradually evolved, and the task was assigned to Irina Blok , a Russian-born illustrator and designer who worked at Google. Her mission was to create a friendly, recognizable symbol that could represent Android around the world.

Irina wanted the mascot to be simple, geometric, and instantly recognizable, even at a glance. This minimalist approach led to the creation of Bugdroid, the Android mascot we know today.

The main inspiration for Bugdroid came from the universal restroom signs commonly found in public places. According to the story, while working at Google's offices, Irina noticed those pictograms and realized that their clean, universal design was exactly the kind of visual language she wanted for Android. The result was a mascot that is simple, memorable, and easy to identify in any language or culture.

WANT TO LEARN MORE ?

Another interesting fact about Android is that each release used to be named after a dessert, following alphabetical order from Android 1.5 to Android 9.0.

  • 1.5 — Cupcake
  • 1.6 — Donut
  • 2.0 — Éclair
  • 2.2 — Froyo
  • 2.3 — Gingerbread
  • 3.0 — Honeycomb
  • 4.0 — Ice Cream Sandwich
  • 4.1 — Jelly Bean
  • 4.4 — Kitkat
  • 5.0 — Lollipop
  • 6.0 — Marshmallow
  • 7.0 — Nougat
  • 8.0 — Oreo
  • 9.0 — Pie

Unfortunately, this tradition came to an end with the release of Android 10. Since then, Google has adopted a simple numerical naming system instead of dessert codenames.
